How to Play: Gin Rummy

Card game · Exactly 2 players · Everyone · $2.99

Goal

The classic 2-player knock-and-meld game. Play a series of hands, scoring points each time, until one player reaches 100.

Setup

A standard 52-card deck is shuffled. Each player is dealt 10 cards. The next card is turned face up to start the discard pile; the rest becomes the stock.

Melds

A meld is either 3 or 4 of the same rank, or a run of 3 or more consecutive cards in the same suit. Aces are low only — there's no wrapping from King to Ace.

Starting the hand

The non-dealer decides whether to take the face-up starting card or pass on it. If they pass, the dealer gets the same choice. If both pass, the non-dealer draws from the stock to begin play as normal — you can never immediately take back a card you just declined.

Playing a turn

  1. Draw one card, either from the stock or the top of the discard pile.
  2. Discard one card face up, ending your turn — or knock instead: discard a card and reveal a hand where your unmatched cards ("deadwood") add up to 10 points or less. Aces count 1, face cards count 10, everything else is face value.

If your deadwood is a perfect 0, that's a gin — you skip straight to scoring, no layoff round.

Layoff

After an ordinary knock (not a gin), your opponent gets to lay off any of their own leftover deadwood cards onto your revealed melds — extending a set, or adding to either end of a run — before deadwood is compared.

Scoring a hand

  • Gin: the knocker scores the opponent's full deadwood, plus a 25-point bonus.
  • Undercut: if the opponent's deadwood ends up equal to or lower than the knocker's after laying off, the opponent scores the difference plus a 25-point bonus, taking the hand instead.
  • Ordinary knock: the knocker scores the difference between the two deadwood totals.

Running out of cards

If the stock runs down to its last 2 cards before anyone knocks, the hand ends in a wall — nobody scores, and the same dealer deals again.

Winning

Hands keep being dealt, with the dealer alternating each time (except after a wall), until a player's total score reaches 100 — they win the match.
