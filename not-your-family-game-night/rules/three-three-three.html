How to Play: 3-3-3

Card game · 3–8 players · Everyone · $3.99

Goal

A fast 3-card poker showdown. Win hands to build your stack, and outlast the table — the game only ends when one player is left standing and beats one final hand dealt to "the deck" itself.

Hand ranking (simplified)

3-3-3 uses a deliberately simple ranking — no straights, flushes, or straight flushes:

  1. Three of a kind (highest)
  2. A pair
  3. High card (lowest)

3s are wild

Every 3 in your hand becomes a copy of your highest other card. A few examples:

  • 3, K, 9 → a pair of Kings (kicker 9)
  • 3, Q, Q → three Queens
  • 3, 3, 7 → three 7s
  • 3, 3, 3 → the best possible hand, tied only with a natural Ace-Ace-Ace

Playing a hand

  1. Every player antes into the pot, and everyone is dealt 3 cards.
  2. Draw round: in turn order, each player chooses any of their 3 cards to discard and draw fresh replacements — or stands pat and keeps their hand. You can also fold here instead of drawing.
  3. Bet round: in turn order, each player checks, bets, calls, or folds, same as standard poker betting.
  4. Once betting is complete, remaining players reveal their hands — best hand wins the pot.

Betting and side pots

You can bet any amount up to your own stack. If you can't cover a bet, you go all-in for whatever you have left, and only compete for the part of the pot you actually covered — real side pots, just like in real poker, so an all-in player can't win chips they never matched.

If you stayed in but didn't win

Any player who made it all the way to showdown without winning any part of the pot has to individually match the full pot amount — that goes into the next hand's starting pot. Folding earlier avoids this.

Beating the deck

Once only one player remains in the game (everyone else has been eliminated or folded out of existence), that lone survivor doesn't just win outright — they face one final hand dealt to "the deck" itself, revealed the same way as a normal showdown. Beat the deck's hand and you win the whole game. Lose to the deck, and you must match the pot just like a losing showdown — but the deck never actually takes the pot, so it carries forward and you keep playing.

Running out of tokens

A player who can no longer afford to ante is eliminated from the game. The game continues with whoever's left until it comes down to that final player-versus-deck hand.
